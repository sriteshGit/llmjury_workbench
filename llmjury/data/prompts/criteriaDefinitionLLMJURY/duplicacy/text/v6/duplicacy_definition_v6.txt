Redundancy: Evaluate whether the mindmap avoids excessive duplication while allowing for natural overlap that may occur in complex topics.

What to look for:
1. Nodes generally represent distinct ideas, though some overlap is acceptable when concepts appear in different meaningful contexts
2. Direct word-for-word repetition is minimized, but related concepts or synonyms are acceptable when they serve different purposes
3. Similar concepts may appear in different branches if they provide different perspectives or contexts
4. Intentional cross-references and thematic connections are encouraged when purposeful
5. Minor overlaps that aid understanding are acceptable

Score 5 (Excellent): Minimal redundancy; nodes add value even when concepts overlap slightly. Any repetition serves a clear purpose (e.g., different context, cross-reference). Mindmap is clear and well-organized.

Score 4 (Good): Some natural overlap exists but doesn't confuse; most content is unique. Related concepts may appear in different branches when contextually appropriate. Overall organization remains clear.

Score 3 (Acceptable): Noticeable repetition but doesn't significantly hinder comprehension. Some consolidation would improve clarity, but the mindmap is still usable as-is.

Score 2 (Poor): Excessive redundancy makes the mindmap harder to navigate. Multiple instances of very similar concepts without clear differentiation clutter the structure.

Score 1 (Very Poor): Severe duplication throughout; identical or nearly identical concepts repeated unnecessarily across many branches. Map requires significant cleanup to be useful.
