# Summary Evaluation Criteria v2

## 1. Accuracy (1-5)
**Definition**: The degree to which the summary precisely and correctly represents the factual information from the source text, without any distortions or misrepresentations.

**Scoring Guide**:
- 5: Perfectly accurate with no factual errors
- 4: Minor factual inaccuracies that don't significantly impact understanding
- 3: Some factual errors that affect key points
- 2: Multiple significant factual errors
- 1: Major factual errors that completely misrepresent the source

## 2. Truthfulness (1-5)
**Definition**: The extent to which the summary contains only information that can be directly derived or reasonably inferred from the source text, without adding unsubstantiated claims or external information.

**Scoring Guide**:
- 5: Contains only information directly from or reasonably inferred from source
- 4: Minor instances of unsubstantiated information
- 3: Some information that cannot be verified from source
- 2: Multiple instances of unverified or external information
- 1: Contains significant unsubstantiated or fabricated information

## 3. Comprehensiveness (1-5)
**Definition**: The degree to which the summary captures all essential information, main ideas, and key details from the source text, providing a complete understanding without significant omissions.

**Scoring Guide**:
- 5: Captures all essential information and key details
- 4: Minor omissions of non-critical details
- 3: Missing some important points but maintains core message
- 2: Significant omissions of key information
- 1: Fails to capture most essential information

## 4. Conciseness (1-5)
**Definition**: The ability to convey information efficiently without redundancy or unnecessary elaboration while maintaining clarity and completeness.

**Scoring Guide**:
- 5: Exceptionally concise with no redundancy
- 4: Mostly concise with minor wordiness
- 3: Some redundancy or unnecessary details
- 2: Significant redundancy or verbosity
- 1: Highly redundant or verbose with poor information density

## 5. Navigation (1-5)
**Definition**: The clarity and logical organization of information, making it easy for readers to follow and understand the flow of ideas.

**Scoring Guide**:
- 5: Perfectly structured with clear logical flow
- 4: Well-organized with minor structural issues
- 3: Some organizational challenges but still readable
- 2: Poor organization affecting readability
- 1: Disorganized and difficult to follow

## 6. Clarity (1-5)
**Definition**: The degree to which the summary is written in clear, understandable language with appropriate terminology and sentence structure.

**Scoring Guide**:
- 5: Exceptionally clear with precise language
- 4: Mostly clear with minor ambiguities
- 3: Some unclear passages or confusing terminology
- 2: Frequent unclear or confusing sections
- 1: Consistently unclear and difficult to understand

## 7. Coherence (1-5)
**Definition**: The logical connection and flow between ideas, ensuring that the summary reads as a unified whole rather than disconnected points.

**Scoring Guide**:
- 5: Perfectly coherent with smooth transitions
- 4: Mostly coherent with minor transition issues
- 3: Some coherence issues but main points connected
- 2: Frequent coherence problems
- 1: Lacks coherence between ideas

## 8. Balance (1-5)
**Definition**: The appropriate distribution of attention and detail across different aspects of the source text, ensuring no single aspect is over- or under-emphasized.

**Scoring Guide**:
- 5: Perfectly balanced coverage of all aspects
- 4: Mostly balanced with minor emphasis issues
- 3: Some imbalance in coverage
- 2: Significant imbalance in coverage
- 1: Highly imbalanced with major aspects neglected
